# CTA Rules

## Contact CTA
Every blog post must end with a call-to-action linking to:

```
https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact
```

### CTA Phrasing Options
Choose the phrasing that fits the post's content and tone most naturally. The anchor text containing the link should always be conversational and grammatically correct within the sentence.

**Standard options (rotate for variety):**
- `[contact me](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)` — default, personal, approachable
- `[reach out](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)` — casual, low-pressure
- `[get in touch](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)` — warm, inviting
- `[let's talk](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)` — collaborative, conversational
- `[schedule a conversation](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)` — professional, action-oriented
- `[send me a message](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)` — direct, simple

### CTA Sentence Examples
The CTA should be a complete, grammatically correct sentence that fits the post's conclusion. Here are examples showing proper integration:

- "If you're tired of watching competitors show up above you in search results, [contact me](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact) and we'll figure out where to start."
- "Want help building a marketing plan that actually fills your route board? [Reach out](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact) and let's have a conversation."
- "If any of this sounds familiar, [get in touch](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact). I'd love to help you put a plan together."
- "Ready to turn your website into a lead generation tool instead of a digital brochure? [Let's talk](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)."
- "Not sure where to start with your company's marketing? [Send me a message](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact) and I'll point you in the right direction."
- "If you want a second set of eyes on your current marketing strategy, [schedule a conversation](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact). No pressure, no pitch. Just honest feedback."

### CTA Rules
- Always use first person singular: "me," "I," "I'd," "I'll" — never "us" or "we" in the CTA link text
- "We" is acceptable in the surrounding sentence (e.g., "we'll figure out where to start") but the linked text itself should feel personal
- The CTA must be a natural conclusion to the post, not a disconnected sales pitch
- Vary the CTA phrasing across posts — do not use the same option in consecutive posts
- The surrounding sentence should reference the post's specific topic, not be generic
- Always check grammar: the linked phrase must read correctly within the full sentence

### One Action, Zero Risk
- **One primary CTA per post.** Give the reader one thing to do. A second link to a different action splits the click.
- **Say what happens next, and remove one risk.** Name the step ("a 20-minute call", "I'll reply within a day") and take away one worry: time, cost, or commitment ("no contract", "free", "no pitch").

## Who-We-Serve Link
Every blog post introduction must include ONE natural link to:

```
https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/who-we-serve/[YOUR_VERTICAL_SLUG]-marketing-services
```

Vary the anchor text based on context. Choose whichever fits the sentence most naturally:
- "[YOUR_VERTICAL] companies"
- "[YOUR_VERTICAL] marketing"
- "[YOUR_VERTICAL] businesses"
- "[SERVICE_PROVIDER]s and [SERVICE_PROVIDER]s"
- "[YOUR_VERTICAL] business owners"
- "[YOUR_VERTICAL] service providers"
- "companies like yours"
- "[SERVICE_PROVIDER]s"
- "independent [YOUR_VERTICAL] companies"
- "local [YOUR_VERTICAL] businesses"

Do NOT force the link — build the sentence around it so it reads naturally. Never use the same anchor text in consecutive posts if possible.

## TL;DR CTA
The last bullet in the TL;DR should reference the action item or CTA, but must NOT contain any links. No links in the TL;DR, ever.
