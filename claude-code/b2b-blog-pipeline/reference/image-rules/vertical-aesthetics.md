# Vertical Aesthetics

Detect the audience from the draft content. Never ask. Pull the matching aesthetic into
both the analysis and every prompt. If the audience is unclear or general, match the
article's own tone exactly rather than defaulting to a genre.

Replace or extend the profiles below with your own verticals. Keep each one to an
audience line, an aesthetic line, what to ground the visuals in, and what to avoid.

## B2B Service Businesses (the default for this template)
- Audience: owners, operations managers and marketing leads at [YOUR_VERTICAL] companies
- Aesthetic: grounded, competent, real work. Professional without corporate gloss.
- Ground visuals in audience-recognizable imagery: the job site, the service vehicle, the
  office or dispatch desk, real equipment, a client handshake seen from behind.
- Avoid: futuristic or cyberpunk looks for tech topics, stock-photo boardrooms, and
  anything that looks staged for a brochure.

## Trades and Field Services
- Audience: owners of HVAC, plumbing, roofing, cleaning, landscaping and similar companies
- Aesthetic: blue-collar confidence, resourceful, practical.
- Ground visuals in vans, tools, crews on a job, a technician at a customer's door.

## Professional Services
- Audience: partners and practice managers at accounting, legal, engineering or consulting firms
- Aesthetic: calm authority, clarity, trust.
- Ground visuals in workspaces, documents rendered as illegible texture, client meetings
  framed from behind.

## Any vertical
- A draft may fall outside the profiles above (medical, retail, SaaS and so on). In that
  case, infer the audience and register from the draft itself and choose composition,
  lighting and style anchors to match. The rules in `composition-standards.md` and
  `style-anchors.md` still apply.
