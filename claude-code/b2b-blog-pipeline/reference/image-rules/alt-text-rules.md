# SEO Alt-Text Rules

Every recommended visual gets alt text. Accessibility first, SEO second — the two rarely
conflict when the description is honest.

- Describe the image accurately for someone who cannot see it.
- Be specific: "Technician checking a rooftop HVAC unit at dusk" beats "a worker."
  "Office manager reviewing a dispatch board" beats "people at work."
- Work in the target keyword or a natural variant ONLY when it fits the actual image. Never
  keyword-stuff.
- Keep it under ~125 characters.
- No "image of" or "picture of" — screen readers already announce it's an image.
- No em dashes. Use commas or periods.
- Match the alt text to what the generated image will actually show, not to the ideal image.

## Placement cadence and purpose

- Recommend a visual roughly every 300–500 words to break scroll fatigue, but placement
  follows meaning, not a rigid word count.
- The first visual sits near the top (above or just below the fold) to hold attention early.
- Charts and diagrams beat stock photos for explaining process or data.
- Original photos (real job sites, real staff, real results) outperform generic stock for
  trust and local relevance. Flag where the writer should source their own rather than generate.
- Do not invent a visual for a section that genuinely needs none.

## Purpose tags (pick one per visual)

- Break up a long stretch of text
- Illustrate a complex idea or process
- Show data (chart, graph, comparison)
- Evoke emotion or set tone
- Demonstrate a product, result, or before/after
