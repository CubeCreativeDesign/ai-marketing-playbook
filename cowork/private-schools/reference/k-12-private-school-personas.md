# Reader Personas — K-12 Private Schools

Reader personas help you write content that speaks directly to the people you're trying to reach. Instead of writing for "school administrators" in general, you're writing for a specific person with specific problems, budgets, and decision-making authority.

## How to Build Your Personas

Create 3-6 personas that represent your actual target audience. Each persona should represent a distinct segment — different school types, budget levels, or roles. The more specific you make them, the better your content will land.

### What to Include in Each Persona

For each persona, fill in the sections below. Use real data from your sales conversations, client intake forms, and industry research. Don't guess — base these on people you've actually talked to.

## Persona Template

Copy this template for each persona you create.

```markdown
# [Role Title] — [Persona Name]

## Demographics
- **Name:** [Give them a realistic name — it makes writing to them easier]
- **Age:** [Typical age range for this role]
- **Education:** [Degree, certifications relevant to the role]
- **Professional Background:** [Career path that led them to this role]
- **Organizational Structure:** [Who they report to, who reports to them]
- **Budget Authority:** [What they can spend, what needs approval]

## Role-Specific Information
- **Job Title:** [Their actual title]
- **Responsibilities:** [What they own day-to-day]
- **Authority Level:** [What they can decide vs. what needs sign-off]

## Institutional Context
- **School Size:** [Enrollment numbers]
- **Type:** [College prep, faith-based, virtual, Montessori, etc.]
- **Tuition Level:** [Annual range]
- **Governance Structure:** [Board, church leadership, ownership, etc.]

## Psychographics
- **Values:** [What drives their professional decisions]
- **Educational Philosophy:** [How they think about education]
- **Professional Aspirations:** [Where they want to go]
- **Work Style:** [How they operate day-to-day]

## Decision-Making Process
- **Evaluation Criteria:** [How they evaluate vendors and tools]
- **Approval Chain:** [Who else is involved in buying decisions]
- **Budget Cycles:** [When they plan and spend]

## Professional Goals and Challenges
- **Goals:** [3-5 specific, measurable goals]
- **Challenges:** [5-7 real obstacles they face]

## Pain Points
- [List the specific frustrations this persona deals with]
- [These should map to problems your content solves]

## Information Sources and Trusted Authorities
- **Professional Organizations:** [Industry groups they belong to]
- **Publications:** [What they read]
- **Events:** [Conferences they attend]
- **Digital Sources:** [Blogs, podcasts, social groups they follow]

## Preferred Communication Channels
- **Professional:** [How they prefer to be reached]
- **Research:** [How they evaluate options]
- **Content Consumption:** [When and how they read content]

## A Day in the Life
[Write 2-3 paragraphs describing a typical day. This helps you understand
when they have time to read your content and what's competing for their
attention. Ground it in realistic details.]

## Test Scenarios (Optional)
[If you have a calculator, ROI tool, or interactive content, create a
test scenario using this persona's realistic numbers. This helps QA your
tools against real-world inputs.]
```

## Example Persona Categories for Private Schools

These are common persona types for agencies marketing to K-12 private schools. Pick the ones that match your client base.

### By Role
- **Marketing Director / Director of Admissions** — Controls marketing budget, evaluates vendors, manages brand. Budget: $50K-$250K. Your primary buyer.
- **Head of School / Principal (Mid-Range)** — Strategic oversight, wears many hats, manages board relationships. Budget: $30K-$75K. Often the final decision-maker.
- **Principal (Lower-Cost School)** — Budget-constrained, mission-driven, hands-on everything. Budget: $5K-$15K. Needs to see ROI on every dollar.
- **Virtual Academy Director** — Tech-forward, national reach, different enrollment challenges. Budget: $50K-$75K. Unique marketing needs.
- **Faith-Based School Administrator** — Church-affiliated, community-focused, smaller enrollment. Budget: $25K-$50K. Balances faith mission with growth.
- **Parent Researcher / Influencer** — Not a buyer, but shapes the conversation. Active volunteer, word-of-mouth driver.

### By School Type
- **Non-denominational college prep** (400-800 students, $18K-$35K tuition)
- **Independent college prep** (200-300 students, $15K-$25K tuition)
- **Faith-based community school** (200-300 students, $3K-$5K tuition)
- **Private online/virtual academy** (250-500 students nationally, $8K-$15K tuition)
- **Faith-based K-8** (150-200 students, $8K-$15K tuition)

## Tips for Writing Effective Personas

1. **Base them on real people.** Think of 2-3 actual clients or prospects who fit each persona. What do they worry about? What did they ask in your first meeting?

2. **Include budget specifics.** "Small budget" means nothing. "$800/month for all marketing" tells you exactly what content will resonate.

3. **Write the Day in the Life section.** It feels silly, but it forces you to think about when this person reads content, what competes for their attention, and what language they actually use.

4. **Update them regularly.** Personas aren't a one-time exercise. Revisit them quarterly as you learn more about your audience.

5. **Use them in your batch queue.** Every blog post in your content pipeline should target a specific reader persona. This keeps your content focused and prevents the "writing for everyone, resonating with no one" trap.

## How These Personas Are Used in the Pipeline

- **Stage 2 (Blog Post Writing):** The writer tailors language, examples, and pain points to the selected reader persona's school type, budget, and challenges.
- **Batch Queue:** Each entry specifies a Reader Persona so content targets a specific audience segment.
- **Practical Application sections:** Examples are grounded in the persona's school profile (size, budget, type) without naming specific schools.
