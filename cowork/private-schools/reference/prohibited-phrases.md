# Prohibited Words and Phrases

This list covers every deliverable: blog posts, titles, meta descriptions, FAQs, TL;DRs, and anything else the pipeline writes. Not every entry is a flat ban. Each section heading carries a tier marker that says how strict it is. A section with no marker is a hard ban.

## How to apply this file

Cowork has no scanner, so Claude applies these tiers by judgment. Read the whole draft against this file in Stage 2 and again in Stage 3. Fix every required item. Note every warning you chose to keep, with the reason, in the draft's Self-Check block.

**Tier key**

- **No marker (hard ban).** Required fix. Rewrite the sentence. These words never appear in the post's own voice.
- **`[residue]` (hard fail).** Text a chatbot leaves behind. Required fix, with one exception: inside quotation marks it is example dialogue and passes.
- **`[unless sourced]`.** Required fix, unless the same sentence names the source. "Studies show" fails. "A 2025 NAIS report shows" passes.
- **`[needs proof]`.** Warning. The claim needs proof in the same sentence or the next one: a number, a named example, a link, or a named source. With none of the three nearby, add proof or cut the claim.
- **`[with stats]`.** Warning, and only in a sentence that already carries a number or a source link. If the source gives an exact figure, use it instead of the vague word. In a sentence with no number, these words are fine.
- **`[limit 1]`.** The first use in a post is fine. Each use after that is a warning. A comparison piece may use more, case by case.
- **`[headings]`.** Checked in headings only (H1 to H3, plus title options). Body copy is ignored. A heading that carries a number passes. A heading that makes an empty promise gets rewritten.
- **`[avoid]`.** Warning. Use only when no plain word fits. The fix is to make the sentence specific, not to swap in a synonym.

**What never counts as a hit**

- Text in a blockquote (a line that starts with `>`).
- A proper noun or a real program name that happens to contain the word.
- Text inside a URL or a link target.
- A direct quote attributed to a named person or source.

**One word, two tiers.** A word can sit in two sections. For example, "unlock" and "delve" are `[headings]` words in a heading and `[avoid]` words in body copy. Apply the tier that fits where the word appears. When two tiers both apply, the stricter one wins.

## Banned Corporate Buzzwords
- "leverage" (use "use" or "take advantage of")
- "utilize" (use "use")
- "robust"
- "synergy"
- "streamline"
- "cutting-edge"
- "holistic"
- "paradigm shift"
- "game-changer"
- "showcasing"
- "testament"
- "vibrant"
- "crucial" or "critical" (say why it matters: a number or a consequence, not a synonym)

## Banned AI-Sounding Phrases
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "In today's digital age"
- "Digital landscape"
- "Play a significant role in shaping..."
- "Significantly enhances..."
- "It is important to note that..." / "It's worth noting that..."
- "landscape" (as in "the marketing landscape")
- "dive into" or "deep dive"
- "navigate" (as in "navigate the challenges")
- "at the end of the day"
- "move the needle"
- "in order to" (just use "to")

## Action Words in Headings [headings]
A warning, checked in headings only. The verb isn't the problem. An empty promise is. "Grow Fall Applications 20% With a Second Open House" passes. "Grow Your Enrollment" gets a warning. A heading that carries a number passes.
- "Unlock"
- "Discover"
- "Boost"
- "Grow"
- "Optimize"
- "Delve"
- "Revolutionize"

## Chatbot Residue (hard fail) [residue]
Inside quotes it is example dialogue and passes: "That is a great question for our admissions director."
Text a chatbot leaves behind. Any hit outside quotes is a required fix. This also catches a template placeholder you forgot to fill in, such as `[YOUR_AGENCY]`.
- "I hope this helps", "great question", "certainly!", "as an AI language model", "as an AI model", "as of my last update", "I can't browse", "I cannot browse", "[company name]", "[insert", "[your"

## Vague Attribution [unless sourced]
A required fix unless the same sentence names the source ("a 2025 NAIS survey found...").
- "experts say", "experts agree", "studies show", "studies have shown", "research shows", "research suggests", "according to industry reports", "industry reports suggest"

## Contrast Frame [limit 1]
One per piece. A comparison piece may use more, case by case. Extra uses are a warning, never a fail.
- "not just", "not only"

## AI-Tell Words and Phrases [avoid]
Use only if necessary, when no plain word fits. These are warnings, never failures. Fix: make it specific. A number, a name, or a concrete detail beats a plainer synonym. Swapping "enhance" for "improve" clears the list and stays vague. When a plain word is all the sentence needs: dig into, show, build, use, start, raise, help, back up, grow, open, smooth, careful, strong, changing, well-known, also, so, still.
- "delve", "underscore", "foster", "harness", "embark", "elevate", "empower", "facilitate", "bolster", "amplify", "enhance", "unlock"
- "pivotal", "vital", "seamless", "multifaceted", "intricate", "meticulous", "groundbreaking", "transformative", "unprecedented", "innovative", "compelling", "ever-evolving", "renowned", "nuanced"
- "tapestry", "realm", "beacon", "cornerstone", "symphony", "interplay", "myriad", "plethora", "a wide range of", "a diverse array of", "your journey", "roadmap to", "roadmap for"
- "moreover", "furthermore", "additionally", "notably", "consequently", "that being said", "in conclusion", "in summary"
- "it could be argued", "generally speaking", "while it is true that", "aims to", "may potentially", "it is worth mentioning"
- "stands as", "serves as", "plays a crucial role", "underscores the importance of", "marks a shift", "sets the stage for"
- "whether you're", "when it comes to", "in the realm of", "let's explore", "let's dive in", "here's the thing"

## Unproven Claims [needs proof]
A warning, never a fail. Any results claim about us, a client, or a tactic needs proof in the same or the next sentence: a number, a named example, or a cited source. "Proven" with nothing behind it asks the reader to take it on faith. With none of the three nearby, it's a warning.
- "proven", "effective", "trusted", "reliable", "more leads", "more calls", "more customers", "more inquiries", "more enrollments", "better results", "real results", "drives results", "gets results"

## Vague Quantities [with stats]
A warning, only in a sentence that carries a number or a source link . When the source gives an exact figure, use it. "37 of 300 companies" is believed. "Many companies" is not.
- "many", "most of", "several", "a lot of", "lots of", "numerous", "countless", "a majority of", "the vast majority of"

## Writer Habits (judgment only)

Don't default to three. AI reaches for triplets ("clear, concise, and compelling"). Use two items or four when that's what the facts are, and save a triplet for when there really are three things.

End a section on its last useful fact, or on one short line that promises the next specific fact ("That's not the expensive part."). Two of those per post at most. Never end on a one-line summary of what the section just said.

Vary sentence length on purpose. Follow a long sentence with a short one. A one-line paragraph is fine for emphasis.

No emoji in blog body copy or headings. Treat one as a warning. A "**Label:** text" bullet belongs only in a list of real named items, like a glossary, not in every list.

"Customer journey" is a real marketing term and stays allowed. The avoid list only targets journey and roadmap used as vague metaphors.
