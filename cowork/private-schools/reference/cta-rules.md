# CTA Rules

**Customize this file:** Replace the placeholder URLs below with your own website URLs before using this pipeline.

## Contact CTA
Every blog post must end with a call-to-action linking to your contact page:

```
https://yoursite.com/contact
```

### CTA Phrasing Options
Choose the phrasing that fits the post's content and tone most naturally. The anchor text containing the link should always be conversational and grammatically correct within the sentence.

**Standard options (rotate for variety):**
- `[contact me](https://yoursite.com/contact)` — default, personal, approachable
- `[reach out](https://yoursite.com/contact)` — casual, low-pressure
- `[get in touch](https://yoursite.com/contact)` — warm, inviting
- `[let's talk](https://yoursite.com/contact)` — collaborative, conversational
- `[schedule a conversation](https://yoursite.com/contact)` — professional, action-oriented
- `[send me a message](https://yoursite.com/contact)` — direct, simple

### CTA Sentence Examples
The CTA should be a complete, grammatically correct sentence that fits the post's conclusion. Here are examples showing proper integration:

- "If you're ready to stop guessing and start seeing results, [contact me](https://yoursite.com/contact) and we'll figure out where to start."
- "Want help building a marketing plan that actually fits your school's budget? [Reach out](https://yoursite.com/contact) and let's have a conversation."
- "If any of this sounds familiar, [get in touch](https://yoursite.com/contact). I'd love to help you put a plan together."
- "Ready to turn your school's website into an enrollment tool instead of a digital brochure? [Let's talk](https://yoursite.com/contact)."
- "Not sure where to start with your school's marketing? [Send me a message](https://yoursite.com/contact) and I'll point you in the right direction."
- "If you want a second set of eyes on your current enrollment strategy, [schedule a conversation](https://yoursite.com/contact). No pressure, no pitch — just honest feedback."

### CTA Rules
- Always use first person singular: "me," "I," "I'd," "I'll" — never "us" or "we" in the CTA link text
- "We" is acceptable in the surrounding sentence (e.g., "we'll figure out where to start") but the linked text itself should feel personal
- The CTA must be a natural conclusion to the post, not a disconnected sales pitch
- Vary the CTA phrasing across posts — do not use the same option in consecutive posts
- The surrounding sentence should reference the post's specific topic, not be generic
- Always check grammar: the linked phrase must read correctly within the full sentence

## Service Page Link
Every blog post introduction must include ONE natural link to your relevant service page:

```
https://yoursite.com/services/private-school-marketing
```

Vary the anchor text based on context. Choose whichever fits the sentence most naturally:
- "private schools"
- "private school marketing"
- "K-12 private schools"
- "independent schools"
- "private school leaders"
- "private and independent schools"
- "private school administrators"
- "school admissions teams"
- "faith-based and independent schools"
- "schools like yours"

Do NOT force the link — build the sentence around it so it reads naturally. Never use the same anchor text in consecutive posts if possible.

## TL;DR CTA
The last bullet in the TL;DR should reference the action item or CTA, but must NOT contain any links. No links in the TL;DR, ever.
