# CTA Rules

**Customize this file:** Replace `[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]` with your own domain, and replace the who-we-serve path below with the URL of your private school service page.

## Contact CTA
Every blog post must end with a call-to-action linking to:

```
https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact
```

### CTA Phrasing Options
Choose the phrasing that fits the post's content and tone most naturally. The anchor text containing the link should always be conversational and grammatically correct within the sentence.

**Standard options (rotate for variety):**
- `[contact me](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)`: default, personal, approachable
- `[reach out](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)`: casual, low-pressure
- `[get in touch](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)`: warm, inviting
- `[let's talk](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)`: collaborative, conversational
- `[schedule a conversation](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)`: professional, action-oriented
- `[send me a message](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact)`: direct, simple

### CTA Sentence Examples
The CTA should be a complete, grammatically correct sentence that fits the post's conclusion. Each example names the next step and removes one worry:

- "If your inquiry numbers stalled after the fall open house, [contact me](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact). I'll reply within a day with the two fixes I'd try first."
- "Want help building a marketing plan that fits your school's budget? [Reach out](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact) for a 20-minute call. No contract, no pitch."
- "If any of this sounds familiar, [get in touch](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact). The first conversation is free, and you'll leave with a short list of next steps."
- "Ready to turn your school's website into an enrollment tool instead of a digital brochure? [Let's talk](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact). It's a 20-minute call, and there's nothing to sign."
- "Not sure where to start with your school's marketing? [Send me a message](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact) and I'll point you in the right direction, free."
- "If you want a second set of eyes on your current enrollment strategy, [schedule a conversation](https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/contact). No pressure, no pitch. Just honest feedback."

### CTA Rules
- Always use first person singular: "me," "I," "I'd," "I'll." Never "us" or "we" in the CTA link text.
- "We" is acceptable in the surrounding sentence (for example, "we'll figure out where to start"), but the linked text itself should feel personal.
- The CTA must be a natural conclusion to the post, not a disconnected sales pitch.
- Vary the CTA phrasing across posts. Do not use the same option in consecutive posts.
- The surrounding sentence should reference the post's specific topic, not be generic.
- Always check grammar: the linked phrase must read correctly within the full sentence.

### One Action, Zero Risk
- **One primary CTA per post.** Give the reader one thing to do. A second link to a different action (a newsletter signup, a download, a second contact page) splits the click. The contact link at the end is the one primary CTA.
- **Say what happens next, and remove one risk.** Name the step ("a 20-minute call," "I'll reply within a day") and take away one worry: time, cost, or commitment ("no contract," "free," "no pitch"). One risk is enough. Stacking three reads like a sales page.

## Who-We-Serve Link
Every blog post introduction must include ONE natural link to your private school service page:

```
https://[YOUR-AGENCY-DOMAIN]/who-we-serve/private-school
```

This is a navigation link that tells the reader who you serve. It is not a second CTA, so don't phrase it as an ask ("contact us about...").

Vary the anchor text based on context. Choose whichever fits the sentence most naturally:
- "private schools"
- "private school marketing"
- "K-12 private schools"
- "independent schools"
- "private school leaders"
- "private and independent schools"
- "private school administrators"
- "school admissions teams"
- "faith-based and independent schools"
- "schools like yours"

Do NOT force the link. Build the sentence around it so it reads naturally. Never use the same anchor text in consecutive posts if possible.

## TL;DR CTA
The last bullet in the TL;DR should reference the action item or CTA, but must NOT contain any links. No links in the TL;DR, ever.
