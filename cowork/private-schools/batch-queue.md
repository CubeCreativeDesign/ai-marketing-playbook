This is a fictional example queue showing how to plan a month of content around a seasonal theme. Replace the topics, keywords, writer and reader personas, dates, and notes with your own. Writer names are the example voices in `personas/writer-profiles.md` (The Wry Strategist = `[WRITER_1]`, The Explainer = `[WRITER_2]`, The Digital Native = `[WRITER_3]`). Reader names like "Your Head of School Persona" stand in for the persona names in your copy of `reference/k-12-private-school-personas.md`.

**Theme:** Retention, re-enrollment, and year-end planning.
**Run mode:** Paste Section 1 into the Cowork chat and say "run this batch." Claude runs Stage 0 plus the 8 content stages on each row, in order, without stopping to ask questions. See `batch-template.md` for the batch rules.
**Cadence:** Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, April 2 through April 30.
**Word target:** Main body only (intro through conclusion). The TL;DR and FAQ don't count. Treat the number as a guideline, not a cap.

## SECTION 3: QC Guardrails

Apply these to every post before you mark it done. Claude records each one in the draft's Self-Check block. You confirm them in review.

1. **No fabricated stats.** Every number is checked against the source page's own text, never a summary of it. Deep-research tools invent statistics and citations, so re-check each one.
2. **True word count.** Measure the main body (intro through conclusion) yourself. The TL;DR and FAQ don't count.
3. **Heading levels.** H1 to H3 only. Self-Check subheadings use `###`, never `####`.
4. **Uncited causal claims.** Stage 7 catches claims like "X causes Y" even when they carry no number.
5. **Unsourced absence claims.** Any claim that a competitor or platform "does not" offer something is checked against that source's own site before it ships.
6. **State program facts.** Check your state's current school-choice program pages before you cite an amount or a rule. An average account value is never a maximum award.
7. **Duplicate check.** Search your own blog for the primary keyword before Stage 1. If a live post covers the topic, refresh it instead of writing a twin.

## SECTION 5: How to Build Your Own Queue

1. **Choose a seasonal theme** using `reference/seasonal-calendar.md`.
2. **Identify 8 to 15 topics** that cluster around that theme. Mark one as the pillar.
3. **Check for duplicates.** Search your own blog for each primary keyword. Turn overlaps into refreshes.
4. **Assign writer personas** from `personas/writer-profiles.md` based on topic type (strategy and data, how-to, or social).
5. **Assign reader personas** from your own set in `reference/k-12-private-school-personas.md`.
6. **Mix content goals.** Not every post should be lead generation.
7. **Vary word counts.** Put a pillar (3,000 to 4,000 words) alongside shorter support posts (1,500 to 2,500 words).
8. **Add research notes** in the Research/Notes column when you have source material or a required data source.

### Gaps and Flags to Watch For
1. **Pillar content:** Mark your hub posts. All other entries in the cluster should link back during Stage 6.
2. **Keyword cannibalization risk:** Watch for overlapping primary keywords. Differentiate them in Stage 1.
3. **Cross-month linking:** Connect new posts to relevant content from previous months during Stage 6.
