# Stage 4: Titles, Meta Description, and Keywords

## Purpose
Generate optimized title options across three tiers, a meta description, and a keyword list for the blog post. Read the full draft from Stages 2 and 3 before you generate anything.

**Output feeds:** Stage 5 accepts the Recommended Titles and meta description (automatically in batch mode) and builds the slug and Publishing Package from them. When the post goes live, the SEO title, meta description, and keywords go into the post's SEO fields in [YOUR_CMS].

**Human check:** You, in the Cowork chat (or the person who runs the project), read the title package at the top of the draft before the post publishes. If a title could not meet its limit, or a number in a title does not match the body count, say so first in the Analysis Summary so that person sees it.

---

## Pre-Generation Analysis

Before you create any deliverables, analyze the draft for:
1. Main topic and subject matter
2. Target audience, based on tone, language, and content
3. Writing style and persona voice used
4. Key themes and concepts
5. Primary value proposition for readers
6. **Every discrete item, tip, step, way, method, or strategy in the content.** Count them. This number must be exact if you use it in any title.

## Title Generation

### Three-Tier Title System
Every blog post needs three related titles that build on each other:

| Tier | Purpose | Limit |
|------|---------|-------|
| **SEO/Google Title** | The title tag that shows in search results | 60 characters max, and about 561 pixels max. Aim for 55 or fewer. |
| **Article Title** | The H1 displayed on the page | 55 to 100 characters |
| **Image Title** | The text set on the cover image, and the image's title attribute | 50 to 100 characters, 110 max |

### SEO Title Pixel Width
Google cuts titles by pixel width, not by character count. About 561 pixels is the safe ceiling at desktop size. You can't measure pixels in Cowork, so use this rule of thumb:
- A title of 55 characters or fewer in normal title case almost always fits.
- Wide letters use more space. Capital W, M, and other capitals, plus many digits, make a 56 to 60 character title run long.
- Narrow letters (i, l, t, f, r) and short words give a little room.
- If a title is 56 to 60 characters and has many capitals or wide letters, shorten it.

### Title Relationship Rules
Each set of three keeps the same core concept, but the titles must be clearly different from each other:
1. **SEO title** = concise, keyword-first, optimized for search. This is what shows in Google results.
2. **Article title** = a DIFFERENT title that expands the concept for people reading the page. Do NOT just add "Your" to the SEO title and call it done. The article title should reframe, add context, or take a different angle on the same topic. It must be clearly different from the SEO title at a glance.
3. **Image title** = the words for the cover image. It is a short, punchy version of the article title's angle. Keep the primary keyword, or its core noun, in it. It must not be a copy of the SEO title.

**The SEO title and the article title must NOT be the same title with minor word swaps.** They should read like two different ways to describe the same post. If someone read both side by side, the difference should be obvious.

**Example of the right relationship:**
- SEO: Enrollment Marketing Tips for Private Schools (45)
- Article: How to Fill Your School's Open Seats Before Enrollment Season Ends (66)
- Image: Fill Your Open Seats Before Enrollment Season Ends (50)

**Example of what NOT to do:**
- SEO: Enrollment Marketing Tips for Private Schools (45)
- Article: Enrollment Marketing Tips to Help Your Private School Fill Seats (64). TOO SIMILAR. It only adds "to Help Your" and "Fill Seats."
- These are the same title with filler words added.

### Generate 5 Sets
Produce 5 complete sets (SEO + Article + Image) with a mix of these title styles:
1. **Question format:** "How Can Private Schools Improve Enrollment with Email Marketing?"
2. **Number/List format:** "7 Email Sequences Every Private School Needs for Enrollment Season"
3. **How-to format:** "How to Build an Enrollment Funnel That Converts More Families"
4. **Statement format:** "The Enrollment Funnel Most Private Schools Are Missing"
5. **Benefit-driven:** "Turn More Inquiries into Applications with Proven Email Strategies"
6. **Curiosity/hook:** "Why Your School's Follow-Up Emails Aren't Converting (And How to Fix It)"
7. **Data-driven:** "Schools Using These 3 Email Sequences See 35% More Applications"

### Best Title Recommendation
After all 5 sets, add a "Recommended Titles" section with the best pick from each tier. Format each recommended title as a heading at its matching level:
- SEO/Google Title → H1 (`#`)
- Article Title → H2 (`##`)
- Image Title → H3 (`###`)

Below each heading, add a short line that names the tier and explains the pick. This format makes each title copy-paste ready, with no labels or formatting to strip.

### Image Title Rules
- **50 to 100 characters, 110 max,** counting spaces and punctuation.
- Base it on the article title's angle. Keep the primary keyword, or its core noun.
- Short words and a clear benefit read best on an image. Put the strongest words first.
- An optional colon can split a bold main line from a short supporting line: `The Tax Credit Starts January 1: Is Your School Ready? (54)`
- Write it in title case, not all caps. No em dashes and no quotation marks.
- Use the same text, without the character count, as the cover image's title attribute.

Write out the full title text with its character count. Do not just point to a number in the list. Give a short reason for each pick. Consider:
- Click appeal (would a school administrator actually click this?)
- Search intent match
- Primary keyword placement (near the beginning)
- Emotional resonance
- Specificity to private schools (avoid generic titles that could apply to any industry)

### Title Rules
- Primary keyword near the beginning of SEO titles
- No clickbait. Deliver on the promise.
- Match search intent (informational, how-to, comparison)
- Active voice only. This is mandatory.
- Use AP title case for all titles, exactly as defined in the Capitalization section of `reference/mechanical-style-rules.md` (the single source of truth). It covers why short verbs, pronouns, and subordinating conjunctions stay capitalized, and why articles, coordinating conjunctions, and short prepositions go lowercase mid-title.
- Do NOT capitalize power words just because they are power words. AP title case governs, not emphasis.
- Never put quotation marks around titles in the output.

### Numerical Accuracy (Critical)
Before you suggest any title with a number:
1. Count the EXACT number of distinct items in the content.
2. Use only the precise number that appears in the content.
3. If the content has 5 strategies, NEVER suggest "7 Strategies" or "10 Strategies."
4. If the content has several points but no clear count, skip the number format.
5. State the exact count you found in the Analysis Summary.

### Headline Techniques
Apply these when you write titles:
- **Clarity and specificity:** Name specific benefits or outcomes ("Increase Inquiries by 30%," not "Get More Leads").
- **Curiosity gap:** Hint at the value without giving it all away.
- **Value:** Answer "What's in it for me?" for the reader.
- **Power words:** Use emotional triggers like "proven," "essential," "mistakes." Check each one against `reference/prohibited-phrases.md` first.
- **Numbers and data:** Use specific numbers when they are relevant and accurate.
- **Conciseness:** Cut every word that doesn't add meaning.
- **Rhythm:** Use alliteration or rhythm when it makes the title easier to remember.

## Meta Description

### Specifications
- **Hard limit: 160 characters.** Never go over. Aim for 115 to 125 characters, so the full text shows on mobile and desktop.
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- Focus on reader benefits and solutions
- Create urgency or curiosity without hype
- Active voice only
- **No phone numbers and no "call us" CTA.** The meta description sells the click to the post. Phone numbers belong on service and contact pages.
- No em dashes. Use semicolons, periods, or parentheses. (The meta gets zero em dashes, which is stricter than the 1 per 300 words allowed in body copy.)
- AP style with the Oxford comma
- Address the reader directly when it fits ("you" and "your")
- Professional but conversational tone
- No prohibited phrases (see below)
- Never put quotation marks around meta descriptions in the output

### Generate 1 Meta Description
Write one meta description with its character count. Make it the strongest option. Do not present alternatives.

### Template Structure
`[Key benefit or hook]. [What they'll learn]. [Why read it now, or a soft CTA to read on].`

### Meta Description Rules
- Never create fictional testimonials or case studies
- Every claim must be accurate and match the draft content
- Use concrete language over abstract ideas
- No number that doesn't match the content exactly
- Focus on the practical benefit to the school

## Keywords

### Process
1. Review the primary keyword from the Stage 1 brief.
2. Confirm it still fits the final content. If the draft shifted focus, flag it and suggest an updated primary keyword.
3. Identify the 10 most relevant keywords for the post, based on the content as written.

### Output Format
Top 10 keywords, ranked by relevance to the content. No categories, no breakdown. Just the best 10:

```
### Keywords
* keyword one,
* keyword two,
* keyword three,
* keyword four,
* keyword five,
* keyword six,
* keyword seven,
* keyword eight,
* keyword nine,
* keyword ten
```

Include a mix of:
- The primary keyword
- Short-tail and long-tail variations
- Question-based phrases (for featured snippet potential)
- Terms that appear naturally in the content

All lowercase, with a comma at the end of each line except the last.

See `reference/prohibited-phrases.md` for the complete list. These words and phrases must never appear in titles, meta descriptions, or any other output from this stage.

## Character Counting Rules
For ALL character counts in titles and meta descriptions:
1. Count every character, including spaces and punctuation.
2. Each space = 1 character.
3. Each punctuation mark = 1 character.
4. Count twice before you display the number.
5. Display only the final number, in parentheses after each title or description.
